### 2.5 Gaps & Caveats

1. **Scarcity of True Two-Sided Index Cards:** While hundreds of bound manuscript cookbook pages and single cards exist in public repositories, true photographed front/back 3x5 index-card pairs are rare in public web archives (archives scan cards face-up and rarely photograph blank or lightly annotated backs). Only `rw-044` (envelope front/back) and `rw-016`/`rw-017` (facing manuscript leaves) provide physical pairing. As noted in Remediation Plan §3.3, multi-card grouping evaluation must primarily wait for the T4 real-user photo intake tier.
2. **Ground Truth Sourcing for Modern Cards:** The real family recipe cards from Wikimedia Commons (`rw-001`–`rw-005`) are ideal 3x5 physical cards with stains, handwriting variations, and ruled lines, but lack institutional transcripts. In WP-R2, trusted human transcriptions must be independently established for these cards before vault encryption.
3. **Palaeographic Drift:** Early modern manuscripts (17th–18th century) exhibit archaic orthography (long s `ſ`, abbreviations `th^e`, archaic weights `drammes`, `scruples`), whereas 20th-century cards use modern units (`tsp`, `cups`). The Top 30 recommended subset balances early modern domestic hands with 19th- and 20th-century American vernacular cursive and typescript.
4. **Institutional WAF Restrictions:** Cloudflare Turnstile blocks automated scraping on `crowd.loc.gov` and `transcription.si.edu/browse`. However, all underlying Library of Congress items and crowdsourced transcriptions (`gdccrowd`) are directly accessible via the standard `www.loc.gov` JSON API and storage service (`tile.loc.gov`).
